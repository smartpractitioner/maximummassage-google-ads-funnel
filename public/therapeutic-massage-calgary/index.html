What is therapeutic massage?

Therapeutic massage is results-focused work aimed at a specific problem, chronic pain, an injury, stubborn tension, or stress your body is holding, rather than a general spa-style relaxation hour. Depending on what you need, it can draw on myofascial release, trigger point, deep tissue, fascial, craniosacral, or lymphatic techniques. The goal is to change what’s actually going on in the tissue, not just an hour off, and to send you home with a plan so the relief holds. Most people leave feeling both relieved and calmer.

Benefits of therapeutic massage

  • Real relief from chronic pain and stubborn knots.

    Targeted, results-focused work reaches the tissue that stretching and rest alone usually don’t.

  • Less back, neck, and shoulder tension.

    The spots most people carry daily strain in respond well when the work is specific.

  • Better mobility and range of motion.

    When muscle and fascia release, joints move more freely, often more than you expected.

  • Support for injury and post-surgical recovery.

    With the right timing and technique, therapeutic work helps tissue heal and rebuild function.

  • A calmer nervous system and better sleep.

    Even firm work downshifts the body’s stress response, especially at a pace your nervous system can settle into.

  • A plan that makes the relief last.

    You leave with simple, doable habits so the work compounds between sessions.

What a session looks like

  1. Quick intake (about 5 minutes).

    We talk through what’s going on, what you’ve already tried, and what you’re hoping for, so the work is aimed at the right thing from the start. No 12-page form, just a real conversation.

  2. Results-focused hands-on work (about 45 to 50 minutes).

    Work matched to your body, myofascial, deep tissue, fascial, or gentler techniques as needed. We check in on pressure as we go and adjust, never just push through pain to prove a point. Side-lying, supine, or prone, whatever suits the area.

  3. An at-home plan you’ll actually do (about 5 minutes).

    Before you leave, you get one or two simple things to take with you. A stretch, a habit, a position to change. Small, doable things that help the work hold between sessions.
